# Homer Directories

## Introduction

Homer Directories is a set of free reference guides published as web pages by
a blind author. Each gathers information that is real but scattered, and puts
it where a screen reader user can find it quickly. They are companions to
Homer Tools, a family of Windows apps by the same author.

The guides cover different subjects but share a purpose and a shape. The
purpose is to save the reader the search: someone has already visited the
sources, checked the links, and written down what was found. The shape is
plain and consistent. Each guide is one web page built from a Markdown file
and organized under headings, so you can move through it by heading. Entries
are in alphabetical order, every link points to a specific page rather than a
home page, and appendixes let you browse the same material a second way, such
as by year or category. Nothing is behind a sign-in, nothing costs anything,
and the Markdown source of each guide can be downloaded and read offline.

The guides are listed below in alphabetical order. Each heading is the name
of a guide and a link to it, followed by a short summary.

## [App Help Guides](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/AppHelpGuides/)

App Help Guides collects the official help documentation of popular apps and
services, rebuilding each as a single guide you can read from top to bottom.
Company help sites are usually a maze of short pages behind a search box,
which is slow going with a screen reader. Gathered into one document with a
table of contents, the same material can be searched at once, read offline,
or skimmed by heading.

## [AudioVault](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/AudioVault/)

AudioVault lists the English-language movies and television shows that have
audio description available from the AudioVault collection, each title
linking straight to its audio download page. Entries give the year, genres,
running time, IMDb rating and page, and a description where one is known.
Appendixes list the movies and the shows again by year and by genre, so you
can find described comedies from a given year without reading the whole list.

## [Blind Authors](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/BlindAuthors/)

Blind Authors lists 59 writers who are blind or have low vision, with 86
books they wrote on their own, each first published in 2000 or later and
available today as electronic text or audio through a working link. Lists of
books by blind authors tend to be short, out of date, or mixed in with books
merely about blindness, so this one names the author, the book, and where to
get it in a form you can read.

## [Blind Creators](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/BlindCreators/)

Blind Creators brings 115 people who are blind or have low vision into one
alphabetical listing, covering everyone who authors books, develops apps, or
makes presentations. It merges the three companion directories, so someone
who both writes and speaks appears once, with every kind of work under a
single entry. Start here for the whole picture, and use the individual
directories for one field at a time.

## [Blind Developers](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/BlindDevelopers/)

Blind Developers lists 32 software developers who are blind or have low
vision, with software each has built, limited to projects still actively
developed in 2020 or later. Aspiring blind programmers are often told the
work is possible without being shown anyone doing it, so this guide answers
with names, projects, and ways to reach the developers listed.

## [Blind Movers](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/BlindMovers/)

Blind Movers covers travel, recreation, and tourism while blind or low
vision. Travel advice written for the general public rarely says which parts
of a trip actually depend on sight, so this guide names the organizations,
tours, and services that plan for blind travelers, and explains what to
expect before you book.

## [Blind Presenters](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/BlindPresenters/)

Blind Presenters lists 36 people who are blind or have low vision and who
lead in time-based media: podcasts, talks and keynotes, video, broadcast, and
film, all from 2010 onward. Speakers are usually found through conference
programs and podcast feeds that are scattered and hard to search, so this
guide brings them together with their notable work in one place.

## [Tech Training](https://JamalMazrui.github.io/TechTraining/)

Tech Training is a directory of 1304 free video and audio presentations that
teach technology and accessibility, drawn from 25 sources and sorted into 20
topic categories. Some people would rather learn from a demonstration,
webinar, or podcast than from written instructions, and material like that is
scattered across channels and archives that are hard to search together.
Every title links straight to the presentation with no sign-in, and entries
give the year, listening time, presenter, and source.
